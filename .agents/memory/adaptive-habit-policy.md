---
name: Adaptive habit plan boundaries
description: Calendar-day journeys, rest days, and why numeric adaptation needs fresh evidence after an accepted change.
---

A habit journey is a 22-calendar-day product challenge, not a scientific promise of habit formation. Cadence determines which of those dates are scheduled; rest dates must not count as missed obligations or affect rewards and streaks.

**Why:** The product offers a fixed-length journey while allowing non-daily habits. Treating all 22 dates as obligations would penalize the user's own frequency choice.

**How to apply:** Keep all calendar dates visible, but derive execution eligibility and consistency from their stored scheduled status. Preserve legacy habits without silently manufacturing historical journey plans or rewards.

Today's stored plan remains authoritative even after accepting a new future plan. Numeric adaptation must use fresh evidence for the accepted plan, rather than repeatedly reusing the same old easy/hard days.

**Why:** Future-only changes intentionally leave today's target unchanged. Using the latest habit settings for today's recording can report a false failure; reusing old observations can repeatedly escalate or shrink targets without new behavior.

**How to apply:** Read each day's own thresholds and goal type for execution and display, apply changes only to future unrecorded dates, and separate current-plan numeric evidence from broader nonnumeric coaching context.
